Jared Celniker, Ph.D.

Applied behavioral scientist · Tempe, Arizona

I'm an applied behavioral scientist with expertise in political and moral psychology and in mixed-methods research. I study how people decide who and what to believe, and I develop ways to help people evaluate information and each other more impartially.

About

Most recently, I was the Research Director at Sandbox, a civic technology startup working to enhance mutual understanding across political divides. I ran measurement design, UX research, market surveys, and in-app experiments end-to-end, and I drove cross-functional product development.

Before Sandbox, I directed the Humility in Inquiry Project at Arizona State University, a $3.4 million John Templeton Foundation portfolio led by Nathan Ballantyne, where I managed a team of 14 across 30+ studies and a program of academic events. Prior to my time at ASU, I spent a year as a Postdoctoral Scholar and Program Analyst at UC Irvine's Center for Assessment and Applied Research. There, I conducted usability research and designed the program evaluation infrastructure for 100+ graduate programs ahead of a successful accreditation review.

I earned my Ph.D. in social psychology from UC Irvine, where I was an NSF Graduate Research Fellow working with Pete Ditto. My research sits at the intersections of political behavior, social epistemology, and moral judgment, with an emphasis on identifying and preventing cognitive biases and motivated reasoning.

My research has been published in venues like the Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, the Annual Review of Psychology, and Science, and it has been covered in outlets such as The New York Times, Harvard Business Review, and The Economist. I've also written for USA Today, Psychology Today, and Scientific American.

I'm always happy to hear from people working on related topics, and I'm available for consulting on study design, statistical analysis, measurement design, evaluation, grant and fellowship writing, and more. Get in touch.
